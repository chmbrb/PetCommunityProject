# Code of Conduct

PetCommunityProject is built for people who care about animals and about each other. This Code of Conduct sets out how we treat one another while building it. It's adapted from the [Contributor Covenant 3.0](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/3/0/), with a more detailed list of what isn't acceptable and an enforcement process suited to a small project.

It covers the project's own spaces (see [Scope](#scope)). People using the platform itself are covered by each instance's terms of service and community guidelines, which are planned for Phase 2 (see [docs/COMPLIANCE.md](docs/COMPLIANCE.md#phase-2-adoption-mvp)).

## Our pledge

We pledge to make our community welcoming, safe, and equitable for all.

We are committed to an environment that respects the dignity, rights, and contributions of every person, regardless of:

- race, ethnicity, color, caste, culture, national or social origin, immigration status, or language and accent;
- age;
- sex, gender, gender identity or expression, intersex status, or sexual orientation;
- pregnancy, parental, marital, or family status;
- disability (visible or not), neurodivergence, or physical or mental health;
- physical appearance, body size, or other physical characteristics;
- religion or belief, or having none;
- socioeconomic position, level of education, occupation, or military or veteran status;
- level of experience, or any other status.

These protections apply to everyone. Hostility toward any group is a violation, whether or not that group is a majority or holds more power: misogyny and misandry, prejudice against young people and against older people, and contempt for any culture or faith are all violations, including our own.

Everyone who participates in good faith and follows this Code of Conduct has the same privileges of participation.

## Encouraged behaviors

Social norms differ, and our words can be read differently than we intend because of culture, background, or native language. With that in mind, we behave mindfully toward each other, including by:

1. Respecting the **purpose of the community**: building a safe, trustworthy platform for people and their pets.
2. Engaging **kindly and honestly** with others.
3. Respecting **different viewpoints** and experiences.
4. Using the **names and pronouns** people ask us to use.
5. Writing **plainly**, so people reading in a second language can follow, and being patient with how others write.
6. **Assuming good faith**, and asking before concluding the worst.
7. **Taking responsibility** for our actions and contributions, including their effect on others, whatever we meant.
8. Gracefully giving and accepting **constructive feedback**, directed at the work, not the person.
9. Committing to **repairing harm** when it happens.
10. Behaving in other ways that promote the **well-being of the community**.

## Restricted behaviors

The following behaviors aren't allowed in our community. Doing them, threatening to do them, or promoting them is a violation. Saying it was "a joke", "ironic", "devil's advocate", or "just a question" doesn't make it acceptable: what matters is the effect, not only the intent.

1. **Harassment.** Ignoring someone's clearly stated boundaries, or giving them unwanted personal attention after they've asked you to stop.
2. **Character attacks.** Insulting, demeaning, or belittling comments aimed at a community member or a group of people.
3. **Stereotyping or discrimination.** Judging anyone's personality, ability, or behavior on the basis of their identity or traits (see [Our pledge](#our-pledge)).
4. **Sexualization.** Behavior that's inappropriately intimate for the community or its purpose.
5. **Violating confidentiality.** Sharing or acting on someone's personal or private information without their permission.
6. **Endangerment.** Causing, encouraging, or threatening violence or other harm toward any person, group, or animal.
7. Behaving in other ways that **threaten the well-being** of the community.

### Examples

These examples show the range of behavior covered, from subtle to severe. The list isn't complete: something not listed here can still be a violation.

**Slurs, hate speech, and hateful symbols**
- Slurs and epithets of any kind, including disguised, misspelled, censored, or abbreviated versions, and slurs aimed at others that the speaker considers reclaimed
- Coded language, "dog whistles", hate symbols, and hateful numbers, memes, or imagery
- Supporting or promoting hate groups, supremacist ideologies, or violence against a group
- Denying or celebrating genocide, slavery, or other atrocities against a group

**Prejudice based on identity**
- Racism, and contempt for or mockery of anyone's ethnicity, culture, religion, customs, or national origin (for example "go back to where you came from")
- Sexism, misogyny, and misandry: treating any sex or gender as less capable, less welcome, or less trustworthy, or generalizations such as "men are…" or "women can't…"
- Hostility toward anyone's gender identity or expression, including deliberately misgendering someone, using a name they no longer use (deadnaming), or questioning their gender
- Homophobia, biphobia, and hostility toward anyone's sexual orientation
- Ageism in either direction: dismissing people as too old to learn, or too young to be taken seriously, or using age as an insult
- Ableism, including using disabilities or medical conditions as insults
- Mocking anyone's accent, grammar, spelling, or name, or their ability in English or another language
- Looking down on anyone because of their income, class, education, or job

**Subtle or "casual" put-downs**
- "Jokes", banter, or memes at the expense of a group, even ones meant affectionately
- Stereotypes, even "positive" ones, and backhanded compliments (for example "you're very articulate for…")
- Assuming someone's role, skill, or seniority from their gender, age, race, or accent, or explaining things to them in a condescending way because of it
- Repeatedly talking over, ignoring, or taking credit from the same person or group
- Gatekeeping: telling people they don't belong, or that their questions are too basic
- Pet names such as "sweetie" or "honey" for people who haven't asked for them

**Sexual behavior**
- Sexual innuendo, double meanings, or sexual jokes
- Comments about someone's body, appearance, or clothing
- Unwelcome flirting, advances, or requests for dates or private contact
- Sexual images, emoji, or reactions, and sexualized avatars or usernames
- Sexualized or manipulated images of real people, including AI-generated ones

**Harassment and intimidation**
- Threats of any kind, including "jokes" about violence
- Revealing or threatening to reveal someone's private information, such as their address, legal name, workplace, or health (doxxing)
- Following someone across platforms, contacting their employer or family, or monitoring their activity after they've asked you to stop
- Organizing or joining pile-ons (dogpiling), or encouraging others to target someone
- Endless bad-faith questions or demands for evidence meant to wear someone down (sealioning)
- Persistently disrupting discussions, reviews, or meetings
- Retaliating against anyone for reporting a concern or helping with an investigation
- Creating new accounts to get around a block or an enforcement action

**Animals**
- Promoting, celebrating, or making light of cruelty to animals
- Posting graphic images or descriptions of animal injury or abuse, unless it's needed for the work, such as designing moderation for that kind of content, and is clearly marked with a content warning

### Other restrictions

1. **Misleading identity.** Impersonating someone else for any reason, or pretending to be someone else to evade enforcement actions.
2. **Failing to credit sources.** Not properly crediting the sources of content you contribute.
3. **Promotional material.** Sharing marketing or other commercial content outside the community's norms.
4. **Irresponsible communication.** Sharing or describing restricted behavior without care. For example, a security report or moderation discussion can quote harmful content when it needs to, but should say what it contains and include only what's necessary.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all of the project's spaces:

- the GitHub repository: issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and commit messages;
- any chat, mailing list, meeting, or event the project runs;
- private messages between community members that start in those spaces;
- when someone officially represents the project elsewhere, for example by using a project email address or social media account, or speaking for the project at an event.

Conduct outside these spaces may also be considered if it threatens the safety of community members, for example harassing a contributor on another platform.

## Reporting an issue

Not every disagreement is a violation. This Code of Conduct also describes norms that help people avoid conflict and limit harm. When something does happen, please report it promptly, even if you're unsure, or if it happened to someone else.

To report a possible violation, email **[REPORTING ADDRESS: to be decided]**. Include what happened, where and when, who was involved, and links or screenshots if you have them. You don't need to have spoken to the other person first. If you'd like an intermediary to help with your report, tell us.

Reports currently go to the project lead, who is the only maintainer. **If your report involves the project lead**, or you'd rather not report to them, use GitHub's [report content](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam) feature instead. Once the project has more maintainers, reports will go to a group, and anyone involved in a report will step aside from handling it.

We will:

- acknowledge reports within **7 days**;
- keep the reporter's identity confidential, sharing it only with their permission or when the law requires it;
- review the relevant messages and logs, and talk to the people involved and any witnesses;
- keep investigations and enforcement as transparent as possible while putting safety and confidentiality first. Enforcement is handled privately with the people involved; telling the wider community may be part of a resolution everyone involved agrees to;
- keep a private record of reports and outcomes, so repeated behavior can be recognized.

If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first. Behavior that may be illegal, such as threats of violence or the sexual exploitation of children, may be reported to the authorities.

## Addressing and repairing harm

If an investigation finds that this Code of Conduct was violated, the maintainers use the following steps, depending on the harm to the people involved and to the community. Steps can be skipped for serious violations: threats, doxxing, sexual harassment, or slurs and hate speech aimed at a person or group can lead straight to a suspension or a permanent ban.

1. **Warning**
   - When: a single incident, or a series of incidents.
   - Consequence: a private, written warning.
   - Repair: for example, a private written apology, acknowledging responsibility, and asking for clarification on expectations.
2. **Temporarily limited activity**
   - When: a repeat of a violation that led to a warning, or a first, more serious violation.
   - Consequence: a private, written warning and a set cooldown period, which may cover particular channels or contact with particular people.
   - Repair: for example, an apology, using the cooldown to reflect on the effect of what happened, and taking care when coming back.
3. **Temporary suspension**
   - When: a pattern of violations that warnings haven't resolved, or a single serious violation.
   - Consequence: a private, written notice of suspension from all community spaces, with conditions for coming back.
   - Repair: for example, respecting the suspension, meeting the conditions, and taking care when rejoining.
4. **Permanent ban**
   - When: a pattern of violations that the other steps haven't resolved, or a violation so serious that the community can't be kept safe with this person in it.
   - Consequence: removal from all community spaces, tools, and channels. Bans are rare and need strong reasons.
   - Repair: none is possible at this level.

These steps are a guideline, not a limit on the maintainers' judgment in the community's best interest. Anyone facing enforcement may explain their side before a decision is final, unless that would put someone at risk. Once the project has more than one maintainer, decisions can be appealed to a maintainer who wasn't involved.
